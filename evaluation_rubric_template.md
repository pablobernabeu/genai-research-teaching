# Fallback note and rubric

Use this page if the app fails or when your group wants to record a fuller reflection
after the session. It does not need attention during the fifteen-minute build.

During the build, use the app to record three things: the result of using a tool on your
problem, one or two caught errors and the line you will give the room at 13:33. That
line may name a limitation, a checkpoint you would require before use or how your field
over- or under-uses AI for this task. The material below supports the fallback and the
debrief.

## If the app will not work

Open a note at hackmd.io, or take the paper sheet from the facilitator, and copy the
headings below into it. Make rough notes as you work. They preserve what happened better
than a tidy account written at the end. Give the facilitator the link or sheet before
the lightning round at 13:33 where possible, or during it at the latest.

> If HackMD will not let you sign up, switch to paper at once. The headings work just as
> well there, and a sign-up is never worth the minutes that troubleshooting it costs.

```markdown
# Group ____ · Track A / B / C / D / Own problem · 9 September 2026

The problem:
The artefact, and the tool that made it:
Errors caught (what happened, how we caught it, where a human must stay):
Our line for the lightning round:

Optional, if minutes remain
Automation–steering map (the phases, what the tool ran, where we steered):
Oversight interwoven or staged, and why:
Field reflection (over- or under-used here, and what we would do about it):

Archive this note publicly? yes / no
```

## The rubric after the session

For a short applied session, 3 is a reasonable result. A sharp caught error tells you
more than an unexamined 5. Use 2 and 4 for work that falls between two anchors.

| Dimension | 1 Nascent | 3 Developing | 5 Robust |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Project definition.** Is the problem, and how you would know it helped, clear? | a vague or toy task, with no success criterion | a problem named, with a rough sense of better | bounded and owned, with a criterion you could test |
| **Technology stack.** Is this the right tool on the spectrum, and why? | one tool by default, with alternatives unconsidered | the choice explained, with some sense of the spectrum | placed deliberately, weighing control, data exposure and effort |
| **Data security and ethics.** Were the red lines held? | sensitive material pasted without thought | red lines mostly held and the data de-identified | red lines held, with disclosure, IP, consent and fairness reasoned |
| **Financial and scalability.** Does the free tier hold? | no thought given to cost or scale | limits noted, with a rough sense of what scaling needs | clear on lock-in and on what breaks first, cost, limits or trust |
| **Human in the loop.** Are there checkpoints, verification and accountability? | the output trusted as it stands | some verification, and one or two checkpoints named | oversight chosen and its weakness offset, with one named owner |

To record the scores, add a line to your note for each dimension with its number and a
sentence saying why.

## Worth a few minutes afterwards

Say what thinking you still had to do once the tool had done the writing, and what would
go if you handed that over as well. Note who gains from working this way and who is left
behind, whether by first or second language, support, career stage or background. Set
down whether you would disclose the use of AI here, and whether you would also disclose
the conditions it offset. Then judge as honestly as you can whether your field is over-
or under-using AI for this kind of task, and what you would want done about it over the
next two years.
