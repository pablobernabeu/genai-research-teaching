# The four project tracks and the rubric at a glance

An overview of the core of Part 2, the applied session, for sharing with organisers
and colleagues. The full working briefs are in
[`project_tracks.md`](../project_tracks.md) and
[`evaluation_rubric_template.md`](../evaluation_rubric_template.md).

In Part 2, each group of five takes one real problem (or a ready example) and works it
through one of four tracks, then examines the result against a five-dimension rubric
and a short societal reflection. Every track works for a research or a teaching task,
and everything runs on free browser tools, with nothing to install and no prior
experience assumed.

## The four tracks

Each track is a different test of where generative AI helps a researcher and where it
must not be trusted. A group picks one.

- A · Methodological Blind-Spot Detector. Turn the tool into a sceptical reviewer of
  your own design, then do the harder step: verify which of its critiques are real,
  which are generic and which are simply wrong. For example, stress-test a methods
  section or an assessment.
- B · Accessible Executive-Function Layer. Build a reusable AI routine for the
  planning, triage and summarising that fills a research week, and decide where the
  scaffolding should stop before it de-skills. For example, turn messy notes into owned
  actions, or tame an inbox backlog.
- C · Rapid Prototyping for Knowledge Translation. Build a small working artefact (an
  explainer, a chart, a simple interactive) from a prompt in minutes, then verify it as
  publication would demand, because such artefacts often look right and are subtly
  wrong. For example, a one-page explainer or a quick visualisation of a finding.
- D · Public Engagement Translator. Produce audience-tailored versions of a public
  finding, then audit them for fidelity: over-claims, lost caveats, false certainty. For
  example, a lay summary, a social thread or a schools explainer.

## The rubric: five dimensions

Groups self-assess against five dimensions, each rated from 1 to 5 (1 Nascent,
3 Developing, 5 Robust). It is a reflection scaffold, so a
thoughtful 3 with a sharp caught error is worth more than an unexamined 5.

1. Project Definition: is the real problem, and its success criterion, clear?
2. Technology Stack: is it the right tool on the spectrum, and why?
3. Data Security & Ethics: were the red lines held, and were the UK GDPR, ethics and
   fairness addressed?
4. Financial & Scalability: does the free tier hold, and what breaks at scale?
5. Human-in-the-Loop: are there explicit checkpoints, verification and
   accountability, with a deliberate map of what is automated against where the human
   steers?

## A short societal reflection (not scored)

Alongside the rating, each group reflects on the bigger picture.

- Thinking versus writing: if the tool did the writing, what thinking did we still do,
  and what might we lose by offloading it?
- Fairness: could this widen or narrow disparities (first versus second language, more
  versus less support, career stage, background)? Who gains, and who is left behind?
- Disclosure: would we disclose using AI here, and would we disclose the conditions it
  offset?
- Disciplinary norms: is our field under- or over-using generative AI for this kind of
  task, and how would we redress it?

## And one spoken insight

Each group closes with a 45-second spoken insight, given without slides. It picks one
of three: the single most significant limitation it found, the most important
human-in-the-loop safeguard it built in or one honest observation about how its field
is over- or under-using AI for the task.
