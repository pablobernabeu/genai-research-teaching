The four tracks: pick one
  • A · Methodological Blind-Spot Detector: turn the tool on your own study or design and verify which of its critiques are real.
  • B · Accessible Executive-Function Layer: build a small, reusable aid for planning, triage or accessibility (for example, messy notes into owned actions).
  • C · Rapid Prototyping (stretch): make a small artefact (a script, a chart, an explainer), then check it. Beware the 'vibe-coding' trap, where it runs and looks right but is subtly wrong. You will not fully verify it in 15 minutes, and the verifying is the point.
  • D · Public Engagement Translator: turn a finding into a lay summary or explainer, then audit it for fidelity (what got distorted?).
  • Own problem: a real, non-confidential problem a member brings. Anonymise or synthesise it first.

Can I paste this? The data check

Walk four questions before anything goes into a free tool:

  1. Personal data? (identifies a living person) → anonymise it first, and check your lawful basis.
  2. Special-category or children's data? (health, ethnicity, beliefs, biometrics) → stop, then synthesise or abstain.
  3. Confidential, unpublished or embargoed? (grants, peer review, NDA, no consent) → stop, since it is not yours alone to waive.
  4. Rights not yours to give away? If unsure, treat it as restricted.

If any of these applies, anonymise, synthesise, abstract up a level or abstain, which is itself a valid finding for the museum of caught errors.
